As part of our 4th-year IBM service design capstone, we designed Arrival to Approval, a multilingual financial education platform that helps newcomers to Canada better understand credit scores, financial systems and building credit history, through accessible learning and AI-supported guidance.

## Problem

This project felt personal to me because I also immigrated to Canada, and I remember how confusing and stressful a lot of financial systems felt at first. Credit scores, loans, credit history and banking expectations are treated like common knowledge. For many newcomers, it can feel like everyone else already understands a system you were never taught how to navigate.

As we researched further, we found that many newcomers struggle to access important financial services because they don't have a Canadian credit history yet. That can affect housing, loans, education and overall financial stability. We also noticed that a lot of financial education resources feel overwhelming, overly formal or hard to navigate for people who are still adjusting to a new country and financial system.

With Arrival to Approval, we wanted to explore how UX design could make financial education feel more accessible, supportive and easier to understand, through multilingual learning, conversational guidance and approachable onboarding.

## Research and insights

Our research focused on the challenges newcomers face in understanding Canadian financial systems, building credit history and finding financial education resources.

### Credit systems can feel unfamiliar and overwhelming for newcomers

Many newcomers are expected to understand Canadian credit systems very quickly, without ever really being taught how they work. Credit scores affect things like housing, loans and financial stability, but many people arrive without knowing how scores are calculated or why credit history matters so much.

Looking deeper, we noticed that many financial education resources assume you already understand banking terms and financial expectations in Canada. That insight pushed us to focus on clearer information, accessible design and more supportive onboarding throughout the platform.

### Financial education often feels intimidating instead of supportive

Another thing that kept coming up was how intimidating financial education can feel when you're still adjusting to a new country. Many existing resources rely on long explanations, formal language and information-heavy layouts that make learning stressful and hard to follow.

This shaped a lot of our design decisions. We wanted the platform to feel simpler, more approachable and easier to navigate, so we broke information into smaller lessons, used more conversational language and added AI-supported guidance throughout.

## Designing around real experiences

As we kept researching, we started thinking more carefully about what navigating financial systems actually feels like for someone adjusting to life in a new country.

### Meet Priya

- Recently moved to Canada for graduate school
- Adjusting to living independently in a new country
- Has savings and a part-time job
- Doesn't fully understand Canadian credit history
- Struggles with approvals for housing and phone plans because she has no Canadian credit score
- Feels overwhelmed comparing banks, credit cards and financial advice
- Finds financial information too formal, or written for people already familiar with the system
- Relies on friends, Reddit or social media because she doesn't know where to start
- Wants to feel financially confident while building a stable life in Canada

## Translating research into design

After mapping Priya's experience, we thought more carefully about how the platform should feel for someone using it for the first time.

1. **Making information easier to understand.** A lot of financial information felt too formal and overwhelming during our research. We wanted to break things into smaller lessons that are easier to follow.
2. **Making the experience feel supportive.** Many platforms assume you already understand the system. We wanted onboarding to feel more guided and less intimidating.
3. **Accessibility and language.** Newcomers come from diverse backgrounds, so we wanted the platform to feel accessible, clear and easy to navigate for a wide range of people.
4. **Helping users feel progress.** Instead of making people feel stressed or behind, we wanted the experience to feel encouraging and build confidence over time.
