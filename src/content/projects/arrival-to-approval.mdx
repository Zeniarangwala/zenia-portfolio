## Why this one was personal

I immigrated to Canada too, and I remember how confusing and stressful the financial system felt at first. Credit scores, loans and banking expectations get treated like common knowledge. When you're new, it feels like everyone else already understands a system nobody taught you.

For our 4th-year IBM service design capstone, our team designed Arrival to Approval. It's a multilingual learning platform that helps newcomers understand credit scores, how the system works and how to build credit history, with AI-supported guidance along the way.

## What we found

Lots of newcomers can't access important financial services because they don't have a Canadian credit history yet. That affects housing, loans, education and overall stability.

We also noticed that most financial education resources assume you already know banking terms and how things work in Canada. They lean on long explanations, formal language and information-heavy layouts, so learning ends up feeling intimidating instead of supportive.

## Designing around Priya

We built a persona, Priya, to think through what this actually feels like. She just moved to Canada for grad school, has savings and a part-time job, and keeps getting rejected for housing and phone plans because she has no Canadian credit score. She doesn't know where to start, so she relies on friends, Reddit and social media.

## Turning research into design

We landed on four things the platform had to do:

1. **Make information easier to understand.** Break things into smaller lessons that are easier to follow.
2. **Make it feel supportive.** Onboarding should feel guided, not like you're expected to already know the system.
3. **Accessibility and language.** Newcomers come from all kinds of backgrounds, so it has to be clear and easy to navigate for a wide range of people.
4. **Help people feel progress.** It should build confidence over time instead of making people feel stressed or behind.
