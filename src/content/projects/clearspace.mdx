I created Clearspace to explore how design can make digital experiences feel less overwhelming and easier to navigate. The project focuses on clarity, structure and accessibility, to better support people with ADHD, dyslexia and different cognitive needs.

## The problem

I've experienced how frustrating it can be when digital products don't consider the different ways people think, focus and process information. When an interface feels cluttered or hard to follow, even a simple task can start to feel overwhelming.

For people with ADHD, dyslexia or cognitive overload, things like dense layouts, unclear hierarchy and too many choices can create stress instead of clarity.

This project came from wanting to explore accessibility beyond technical standards, and to design an experience that feels calmer, more intuitive and easier to move through.

## Understanding cognitive overload

What the research found:

- Cluttered layouts, dense interfaces and inconsistent navigation can make information harder to process.
- Many digital products are still designed around “average” users, even though neurodiverse people may process information differently.
- Clearer hierarchy, simpler layouts and shorter sections can help reduce cognitive overload and improve usability.
- Research on decision fatigue showed that constant choices, notifications and interruptions can make digital spaces feel mentally exhausting over time.

Sources: Earth Coaching, UXPA Magazine, “Cognitive Load Optimization in User Interface Design for Info Services”, and “Decision Fatigue in the Digital Era”.

## Design principles

1. **Keep things simple.** I tried not to add features or visuals just because I could. Keeping things simple helped reduce visual overwhelm and made it easier to focus on one task at a time.
2. **Structure makes things easier to follow.** I wanted the interface to feel structured and consistent, so you don't have to keep stopping to figure out where things are or what to do next.
3. **Make things easy to read.** I focused on spacing, hierarchy and readability so the interface feels less mentally tiring and easier to move through.
4. **Guide attention instead of competing for it.** Instead of everything demanding attention at once, the design guides you through the experience with hierarchy, spacing and small visual cues.

## Typography

I realised how much typography affects readability and focus. I wanted the text to feel clear, open and easy to move through, so the interface felt less overwhelming to process.

- **Body font, Atkinson Hyperlegible:** it felt really clear and easy to read. Some fonts started to feel tiring after a while, but this one felt more open and easier to follow, especially in text-heavy sections.
- **Heading font, Plus Jakarta Sans:** clean and modern without feeling too heavy. It creates structure while keeping the interface light and easy to read.
- **Line length, 55–75 characters:** long lines started to feel harder to focus on. Shorter lines made the interface easier to scan and less overwhelming.
- **Line height (body), 1.7×:** more breathing room, so everything feels less crowded.
- **Letter spacing, +0.01–0.04em:** slightly increased to help with clarity.

## Colour palette

Colour can quickly change how an interface feels to use. I wanted this palette to feel calm, soft and easy on the eyes while still guiding attention. I avoided overly bright colours and focused on balance, so the interface supports the content instead of competing with it.

- **Background #EDF1F7:** a softer off-white instead of pure white, because bright white screens started to feel harsh after a while.
- **Text primary #1A2B3C:** the main text colour. Dark enough to stay readable, but softer than pure black so it doesn't feel too intense.
- **Text secondary #3D5468:** for smaller text like labels and descriptions. It separates information without making everything feel too heavy.
- **Blue #0074A5:** buttons and important actions. It stands out enough to guide attention without taking over the screen.
- **Sage #0B764D:** positive actions and confirmation states, because it feels softer and calmer than a bright green.
- **Amber #BE7C1C:** when something needs attention or checking. It catches the eye without feeling stressful or harsh.
- **Border #D3DCE8:** kept really light, so content feels separated and organised without making the interface busy.

## UI components

This part of the project looks at how small UI decisions affect usability. Even simple things like buttons, labels and spacing can change how easy something feels to move through.

I designed buttons to feel clear and easy to understand. The main action stands out, while less important actions stay visible without competing for attention.

## Applied example

Here's a real example of how these ideas come together. I took a standard account creation form and explored how small design choices can make something feel easier or more overwhelming to use. The goal wasn't to change what the form does, but how it feels to move through it.

<Figure
  src="/images/clearspace/form-before.png"
  width={672}
  height={1110}
  alt="Before: a single “Create Account” screen with many fields stacked together, red error text and one Submit button."
  caption="Before: everything on one screen."
/>

What wasn't working before:

1. Pure white backgrounds can feel harsh on the eyes. The contrast is very strong and can be uncomfortable for some people.
2. Labels aren't very clear or specific, so you may have to pause and think about what each field is asking.
3. All the fields appear at once, which is a lot to take in. There's no clear starting point.
4. Errors rely only on red, which doesn't work for everyone, especially people with colour vision differences. There's also no clear guidance on how to fix the issue.
5. The consent text is small and placed at the end, so it's easy to skip over without really reading it.

In the updated version, I redesigned the form into a step-by-step flow, so you only focus on one thing at a time. Each screen has a clearer purpose, which makes the process feel more guided and less overwhelming.

I also added visible progress indicators, clearer labels, helper text and real-time feedback, so you always know where you are and what to do next.
