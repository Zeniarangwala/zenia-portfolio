## The Greyhound we know

Greyhound started in 1914 in Minnesota as a small bus service, and Flix bought it in 2021. Its whole mission is affordable long-distance travel, getting people from one city to another.

The chrome dog and script wordmark are on everything, but the brand still feels old, grey and “cheap bus”.

## Who rides

Real people with real budgets. Riders are mostly young adults (18–24) and seniors (65+) on low to middle incomes, and they need to travel long distances for cheap.

I built three personas from that, and they became my three target audiences:

- **Aditi, 21, a university student** who needs a travel option that fits her budget and is easy to figure out.
- **Liam, 28, a solo traveller** who wants the trip to feel easy and worth taking.
- **Mariana, 38, a mom** who just wants travelling with her kids to feel simple and dependable.

## The gap

Everyone else is selling a seat too. Megabus is cheaper because there are no stations, but you wait on the curb in any weather. Peter Pan is cleaner and more on time, but only on the East Coast. Amtrak is comfy with lots of legroom, but much more expensive.

What I noticed: nobody owns the *experience* of the ride. That was the gap.

My first SWOT had a lot of points. The one that stuck out was under Opportunities: **turn the journey itself into part of the experience.** That line ended up being the whole project.

## The challenge

How can Greyhound keep its history and prices, but feel like a trip people actually want to take?

- Old story: “Greyhound is the cheap way to get there.”
- New story: “Greyhound lets you go somewhere.”

Affordable becomes accessible. Basic becomes spontaneous. Old becomes iconic. A bus ride becomes a road trip, and strangers become human connection.

## The big idea: every journey has a story

Once I knew the problem, I looked for a feeling, and landed on the American road trip, the kind people actually daydream about.

Greyhound has been connecting people and places for over 100 years. My rebrand makes the journey just as meaningful as the destination: the places you see, the people you meet and the stories you take home.

My first campaign lines were “See what's out there.”, “Good stories start somewhere.” and “There's more to the journey.”

My first moodboard (sage greens, burnt orange, the chrome logo and a map) felt flat. Moving to open roads, desert, the coast, bus windows at sunset and Route 66 is where it clicked.

## Identity

Colour, type and logo. Lots of versions, lots of feedback, and one rule I never broke: don't change the dog.

### Logo: same dog, three tries

1. **Try 1:** the chrome dog on sunset backgrounds with italic type. It still looked like the old brand.
2. **Try 2:** the dog in a blue-to-orange sunset gradient with a blue wordmark.
3. **Final:** a cream dog and wordmark on Majestic Mountain Blue. Simpler, warmer, and it works on everything.

The critique changed everything that came after:

- **It's a bus, not a plane.** No gates, no boarding pass.
- **Make the printed ticket real:** black and white, letter size, something you'd actually print at home.
- **Fewer words** and no fake drop shadows.
- **Add the brand voice** to everything.
- **Think about the third audience:** moms travelling with kids.

## Outcome and reflection

The tagline I ended on: **the journey is part of the destination.**

- **Keep what works.** The dog was the strongest thing Greyhound had. Not touching it made every other change easier to trust.
- **Specific beats pretty.** Leaf shadows were nice, but peaches, boots and palm trees actually tell you where you are.
- **Feedback made it real.** Most of my best moves came from critique: the paper ticket, the overlays, the brand voice and the colouring books.
